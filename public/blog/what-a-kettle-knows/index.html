03 · signal

What actually reached the microphone

[SECTION INTENT — Ground the three acoustic regimes in physics. A boil is a sustained, broadband, slowly decaying texture. A pour is turbulence with a broad spectrum and no clear pitch. A spoon tap is a short transient with material-dependent resonances. The spectrograms should make the difference obvious before the prose does.]

the one sentence to land

[CALLOUT — “A kettle is easy to hear and hard to describe: one long event with no fixed boundary, made of the same broadband noise as a hundred other machines.” This is why the next section is about representation, not detection.]

04 · resolution

What survives when you throw the sound away

[SECTION INTENT — Compression is an intuition pump for representation. Keep the same 20 seconds, drop the sample rate and the bitrate, and ask what the ear still needs. The kettle survives at 8 kHz; the material of the spoon does not. This sets up “what should an embedding preserve?”]

128 kbps · 16 kHz
[NOTE — What changes first: the high-frequency fizz of the boil, then the attack of the switch. The low-frequency rumble and the on/off boundaries survive. Ask the reader which of those the task actually needs.]

05 · representation

Which of these sounds are the same?

[SECTION INTENT — The conceptual centre. Four short recordings: the same mug tapped softly and hard, a glass clink, a metal bowl strike. Ask the reader to order them by similarity before showing any model. Then reveal the real cosine matrices. A good representation puts the two mug taps closest and keeps material identity; a bad one collapses everything.]

the question the matrix asks

[CALLOUT — “Same object, different force” is the invariance we want. “Same force, different material” is the contrast we want. A representation is a choice about which differences matter. Every downstream result in this post is a consequence of that choice.]

06 · separation

Can you isolate the spoon from the water?

[SECTION INTENT — A cheap, audible decomposition. Harmonic-percussive source separation splits the cup recording into a steady watery layer and a transient spoon layer. It is not a learned separator, but it makes the point: the mixture is a sum of causes, and a model can choose which layer to attend to.]

HPSS · real decomposition

Cup, spoon and hot water

[INTERACTION — Three synchronised players: mixture, harmonic, percussive. Solo and mute. The reader should hear the water disappear when the harmonic stem is muted, and the spoon disappear when the percussive stem is muted. Link to BASS / MIT for the learned version.]

07 · detection

When did it happen?

[SECTION INTENT — Move from clip labels to time. The ear finds the switch click, the boil onset and the off click, but assigns them the wrong names. Show the predicted episodes against the four human states, then the G1 change result: DSP and SSL beat tagging CNNs on scene change.]

08 · recurrence

How many times?

[SECTION INTENT — Counting is the failure that surprises people. Every embedding path collapses on F2 (0.00–0.14); waveform cross-correlation gets 0.94. Use the bouncing ball as a controlled, visible example: the impacts are evenly structured, and the count is trivial from the waveform but not from a pooled embedding.]
