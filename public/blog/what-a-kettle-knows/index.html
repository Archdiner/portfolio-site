How should a model represent a kettle?

One recording, from the switch on to the click off. Five methods for turning it into something a model can take, and where each one stops.

Asad Rizvi · 14 min read · electric kettle · 1:50 · 16 kHz

The kettle switches on, heats, boils, and switches itself off. Each section below is one way to represent that sound. The measurements are the example inside the method.

01 · the waveform

What is a second of this kettle?

Press play. The label is the recording: an electric kettle, switch on, boil, auto-off.

the kettle · CC0 · richwise Electric kettle · switch on → boil → auto-off
1:50 · 16 kHz · 16,000 samples a second click to seek · space to play

The wave is the samples. Under it, a spectrogram of the same recording, low frequencies at the bottom. You can see the click, the rise, the long boil, and the click off.

At 16 kHz, one second of this file is 16,000 samples. The clip is 1:50, which is 1,760,000 samples. A language model takes text as a short sequence of tokens. It cannot take this sequence the way it takes text.

The waveform keeps the count, the clicks, and the boil. It is also the wrong size to read as text. The next methods each throw some of it away, on purpose.

A longer take of making tea is in the sources table. This page follows the kettle, because that take opens with room tone.

02 · throwing samples away

What survives at 8 kHz, and at 4?

The same twenty seconds of the kettle, three times. Play them in order and listen for what goes first.

128 kbps · 16 kHz

Same stretch of the boil. 16 kHz, then 8 kHz, then 4 kHz.

This is ordinary lossy compression. Cutting the sample rate from 16 kHz to 8 kHz, then to 4 kHz, drops the high frequencies first. The fizz of the boil thins. The click gets softer. The low rumble, and the moment it starts and the moment it stops, are still there. You can throw that much away and still recognise the kettle.

A cut like this does not explain why a learned encoder keeps one distinction and drops another. It only shows that some of the samples were never needed for you to know what you were hearing.

03 · a learned embedding

Which of these sounds are the same?

Four taps. Two are the same mug, once soft and once hard. Then a glass, then a metal bowl. Play them before you read the grid.

The grid is cosine similarity between clip embeddings. Three families of listener show up from here on. dsp_rich is a hand-built DSP baseline, with no learned weights. mn04, mn10, and mn20 are compact classifiers trained on AudioSet. BEATs, EAT, Dasheng, and USAD are self-supervised models. This grid uses the baseline and three of the self-supervised models. The AudioSet classifiers are on the count chart below.

Distance is a choice. Same mug, different force, should stay close. A different material should not. In every model here the mug pair is the closest: 0.96 for the hand-built baseline, 0.98 for BEATs, 0.99 for EAT, 0.97 for USAD. The baseline also puts a soft mug tap furthest from the metal bowl, at 0.56. EAT sits between 0.96 and 0.99, so the materials almost disappear. Four clips. This is not a benchmark. The rate cut above did not make this choice.

An embedding is one vector for the whole clip. Counting is where that stops. A bouncing ball, with the impact times known. Hide the marks, play it, and count them off the wave. The bars are the same question — how many times — asked of the encoders, on 345 clips.

04 · episodes

What happened, and when?

state timeline

The kettle’s 1:50

off · 0:00

Off, then heating, then boiling, then off. Heating runs from 0:02.7 to 0:12.7, ten seconds. Boiling runs from 0:12.7 to 1:39, which is 86.3 seconds. The readout follows the playhead.

This is a different job from reconstructing the waveform, and a different job from a clip embedding. An embedding is one vector for a whole clip. An episode is a stretch with a start and an end. Viterbi is the step that turns per-frame scores into one sequence of episodes. The states on the strip were marked by hand. The lane is the ear’s sequence for the same audio.

What this representation discards is everything that was not stored as an event or a state. If a sound was never written down, it is gone.

05 · what another model can be given

What can another model be given?

Once the sound is a representation, another model has to receive it. Three interfaces are different methods. This page demonstrates only the third.

A continuous embedding is the vectors in the grid above. Another model would have to read them as numbers. Nothing here hands those vectors on.

Discrete audio tokens are the codec path: the wave becomes a sequence of codes, and a language model can be trained to continue that sequence. That is what the Kyutai explainer is about. This page does not demonstrate it.

Tool results computed from a timeline are a count, a time, a duration, or whether it is on. The seven questions are that interface. Each answer is arithmetic on the strip. Nothing in the list calls a language model.

The fields are the ones the listener exposes: how many, how long ago, how long in total, and whether it is on.

The model on the other side of these tools cannot hear anything the timeline did not store. If the boil was never written down as a state, no question can recover it.

What this kettle does not settle

Voice, and the rest of paralinguistics, is the wider ambition. This kettle has no speech in it. A timeline of a boil does not hear a voice, and a voice model does not answer how long the kettle boiled.

the quiet parts

846invented words / hour, no gate
0invented words / hour, speech gate on
0.254 → 0.039word error at 10 dB, after the gate

Whisper is a speech recogniser. Asked to transcribe stretches with no speech, and with no gate, it invents 846 words an hour, and the word error is 0.254. A voice-activity gate — VAD, keeping only the frames where someone is speaking — takes the invented words to 0 and the word error to 0.039. The gate decides when the recogniser runs. It does not change the recogniser.

glossary

RTF. Real-time factor: compute time divided by audio time. Below 1 is faster than playback.

Episode. A stretch the ear marks as one event, with a start and an end.

Onset. The start time of an event.

G1. A test of whether a model notices that the scene changed. F1 is that change score. F2 is the count. F4 is the properties of the change.

DSP baseline. Hand-built signal features, no learned weights. On the charts it is dsp_rich.

AudioSet classifiers. Compact models trained on AudioSet: mn04, mn10, mn20.

Self-supervised models. BEATs, EAT, Dasheng, USAD.

Viterbi. The step that turns per-frame scores into one sequence of episodes.

NCC. Normalised cross-correlation of the waveform. The counting chart calls this waveform correlation.

CREMA-D. Acted emotional speech. Actor-disjoint means the test speakers were not in training.

EPIC dense. A frame-level score on the EPIC kitchen clips, 21 classes.

EPIC vocab. A second score on those clips, over a fixed vocabulary.

Sealed set. A test set opened once, inside the runner.

what this post does not claim

The memory numbers are one home. CREMA-D is fixed sentences, with actors held out. EPIC is kitchens. The cost numbers are fp32, not quantised, and not a phone. The four-tap grids are four clips, not a benchmark. Where the encoders disagree, the page reports the scores and leaves the cause open when the measurement does not decide it.

cost

What it costs to leave it on

One core, the hours you choose, the encoder you pick. The month below is 30.4 days, one clip at a time, fp32.

—core-hours / month
—share of one always-on core
—vs BEATs on the same audio

At 24 hours a day, the share of one core is the real-time factor itself. mn10, the default, is 0.00583, which is 0.6% of a core that never sleeps. BEATs on the same audio is 0.03943, and 141.9 seconds of compute per hour of audio. The smallest AudioSet model, mn04, is 11.3 seconds. The full path is heavier than the encoder alone. It adds a vocabulary and the Viterbi pass from the episode section. That path runs at real-time factor 0.0698, about 251 seconds per hour of audio, and peaks at 3.56 GB. The lightest runs use 0.64 GB, including the DSP baseline with no learned weights, so that figure is mostly the runtime. No dollar price: it moves with the machine.

the ledger

What didn’t work

Each row is an idea that was measured. Read the verdict before the number. Fail, void, and inconclusive are not the same word.

Void means the test did not measure what it was for. The same-home run went from 0.746 to 0.783, and it is void because that home was not held out. Inconclusive means the comparison did not finish: on the fine-tune, the fold-2 validation loss was still falling at epoch 4, with 0.466 against a frozen 0.438. The count row is the observation from the embedding section: embedding scores between 0.00 and 0.14, waveform correlation at 0.940.
