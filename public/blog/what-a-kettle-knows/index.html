02 · recognition

You know what it is. The model has a different vocabulary.

The gap between you and the model is not perception. It is naming. Asked what is happening, you say “making tea” — an object, an action, a goal and a social script in two words. Asked the same question, the ear offers the only vocabulary it has: a light switch, running water, a coffee machine, a vacuum cleaner.

human label

making tea

None of those four ingredients is in the audio. “Making tea” is a label about a person, a kitchen and an intention. The model’s labels are about sounds.

Ear v1 · 15 home classes

The ear was trained on home sound events, not rituals. It has no class for kettle or tea, so the switch becomes a light switch and the boil becomes a vacuum cleaner. This is not a failure of hearing. It is a mismatch of vocabularies — and the first hint that a useful listener needs more than a classifier.

03 · signal

What actually reached the microphone

Play each one. The top picture is the waveform. The bottom picture is a spectrogram, low frequencies at the bottom.

The long one is a boil. It is broadband, it has no clear pitch, and it slowly gets quieter as the water settles. The pour is turbulence: also broadband, also without a pitch, and over in a few seconds. The spoon is a short hit. What rings afterwards depends on the cup. The boil is easy to hear and hard to separate from other machines that make the same kind of noise. Hearing that difference is not yet a decision about which part of it to keep.

04 · resolution

What survives when you throw the sound away

The same twenty seconds, three times. Play them in order and listen for what goes first.

128 kbps · 16 kHz

At 8 kHz the high end of the boil thins out first. At 4 kHz the click is softer, and the low rumble is still there, along with the moment it starts and the moment it stops. You can throw that much away and still recognise the kettle. A cut like this does not explain why a learned encoder keeps one distinction and drops another. That is a choice, and it is the next section.

05 · representation

Which of these sounds are the same?

Play the four. Two are the same mug, once soft and once hard. Then a glass, then a metal bowl. Decide which pair belongs together before you read the grid.

The two mug taps are the same object at two forces. The glass and the metal are different materials. A learned representation makes a choice about which of those differences to keep. In every model here the mug pair is the closest. How far the metal sits from that pair is where they disagree. The hand-built baseline leaves the most room. EAT leaves almost none. Four clips are an illustration, not a benchmark. The previous section only showed that the kettle can survive a lossy cut. It did not make this choice.

06 · separation

Can you isolate the spoon from the water?

One recording, split into three players on one clock. Press play, then mute a row.

HPSS · real decomposition

Cup, spoon and hot water

Mute the steady row and the water drops away. Mute the transient row and the spoon drops away. The split is harmonic–percussive source separation, HPSS: time–frequency bins sorted into a steady layer and a short layer, then rebuilt as two signals. It is a filter on this recording, not a model that learned what a spoon is. You can hear that the mixture is more than one cause. Which cause a learned encoder keeps is a separate question, already visible in the four taps.

11 · speech, once

The same ear on a voice

One chart for emotion in the voice, then three numbers from the quiet parts of a recording.

the quiet parts

846invented words / hour, no gate
0invented words / hour, speech gate on
0.254 → 0.039word error at 10 dB, after the gate

Whisper is a speech recogniser. Asked to transcribe stretches with no speech, and with no gate, it invents 846 words an hour, and the word error is 0.254. A voice-activity gate — VAD, keeping only the frames where someone is speaking — takes the invented words to 0 and the word error to 0.039. The gate decides when the recogniser runs. It does not change the recogniser.

12 · cost

What it costs to leave it on

One core, the hours you choose, the encoder you pick. The month below is 30.4 days, one clip at a time, fp32.

—core-hours / month
—share of one always-on core
—vs BEATs on the same audio

At 24 hours a day, the share of one core is the real-time factor itself. mn10, the default, is 0.00583, which is 0.6% of a core that never sleeps. BEATs on the same audio is 0.03943, and 141.9 seconds of compute per hour of audio. The smallest AudioSet model, mn04, is 11.3 seconds. The full path is heavier than the encoder alone. It adds a vocabulary and a Viterbi decode — the step that turns per-frame scores into a single sequence of episodes. That path runs at real-time factor 0.0698, about 251 seconds per hour of audio, and peaks at 3.56 GB. The lightest runs use 0.64 GB, including the DSP baseline with no learned weights, so that figure is mostly the runtime. No dollar price: it moves with the machine.

13 · the ledger

What didn’t work

Each row is an idea that was measured. Read the verdict before the number. Fail, void, and inconclusive are not the same word.

Void means the test did not measure what it was for. The same-home run went from 0.746 to 0.783, and it is void because that home was not held out. Inconclusive means the comparison did not finish: on the fine-tune, the fold-2 validation loss was still falling at epoch 4, with 0.466 against a frozen 0.438. The count row is the observation from earlier: embedding scores between 0.00 and 0.14, waveform correlation at 0.940.

14 · open

What this does not settle

The kettle is one object in one room. The questions above — what it is, how many times, how long, what happens next — are answered for this recording and for the sets named on each chart. They do not pick an ear to leave running, and they do not say what a second home would look like.

method

Three seeds, or a confidence interval that is stated with the number. Sources in the test are held out. Thresholds, calibration, and the test pass go through one path. A sealed set is opened once, inside the runner, not while exploring. The commands are rx new, rx check, rx run, and rx verdict. glossary

RTF. Real-time factor: compute time divided by audio time. Below 1 is faster than playback.

Episode. A stretch the ear marks as one event, with a start and an end.

Onset. The start time of an event.

G1. A test of whether a model notices that the scene changed. F1 is that change score. F2 is the count. F4 is the properties of the change.

DSP baseline. Hand-built signal features, no learned weights. On the charts it is dsp_rich.

AudioSet classifiers. Compact models trained on AudioSet: mn04, mn10, mn20.

Self-supervised models. BEATs, EAT, Dasheng, USAD.

Viterbi. The step that turns per-frame scores into a single sequence of episodes.

NCC. Normalised cross-correlation of the waveform. The counting chart calls this waveform correlation.

HPSS. Harmonic–percussive source separation. A filter that splits a mixture into a steady layer and a transient layer.

CREMA-D. Acted emotional speech. Actor-disjoint means the test speakers were not in training.

EPIC dense. A frame-level score on the EPIC kitchen clips, 21 classes.

EPIC vocab. A second score on those clips, over a fixed vocabulary.

Sealed set. A test set opened once, inside the runner.

what this post does not claim

The memory numbers are one home. CREMA-D is fixed sentences, with actors held out. EPIC is kitchens. The cost numbers are fp32, not quantised, and not a phone. The four-tap grids are four clips, not a benchmark. Where the encoders disagree, the page reports the scores and leaves the cause open when the measurement does not decide it.
